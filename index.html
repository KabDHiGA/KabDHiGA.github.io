01 · About

Economics informed by mathematics, data, and institutions.

My academic and professional work sits at the intersection of quantitative economics, education, and international development.

I am a PhD student in Economics at Kobe University, with graduate training in economics and applied mathematics.

My research focuses on the economics of education, particularly how household shocks and coping responses affect education expenditure. I am interested in empirical strategies that connect household-level evidence to policy questions about resilience, inequality, and human-capital investment.

Alongside research, I have worked across international development and education settings, including the ILO in Geneva, UN DESA in New York, Education Cannot Wait/UNICEF, and mathematics education in Japan.

02 · Research

Household vulnerability and investment in education.

My current doctoral work examines how shocks alter household education spending and whether coping mechanisms moderate those effects.

Doctoral research · Current

Household Shocks, Coping Mechanisms, and Education Expenditure in Kenya

This research investigates the influence of household shocks on primary and secondary education expenditure, with particular attention to shock type, severity, coping mechanisms, socioeconomic heterogeneity, and regional differences.

The project draws on household survey data and combines economic theory with applied econometric analysis to study how families protect—or adjust—human-capital investment when resources are disrupted.

DataKenya Integrated Household Budget Survey (KIHBS) 2015/16
OutcomesPrimary and secondary education expenditure
Empirical toolsOLS · IV/2SLS · Survey weights · Heterogeneity analysis
TheoryConsumption smoothing · Human capital · Household decision-making
