Discovering Counting Principles

Student worksheet · Seven counting cases · GDC method

Name: ________________________ Class: __________

Decide whether order matters and repetition is allowed. Use your GDC for nPr and nCr; no factorial expansions are required.

1. Order matters — repetition allowed

Roll a six-sided die three times. Are (1,2,3) and (3,2,1) different? Can numbers repeat? Use N = nr. Calculate 63.

2. Order matters — repetition not allowed

Choose a president and vice-president from A, B, C and D. Does swapping roles change the result? Can someone hold both roles? Use 4 nPr 2.

3. Order does not matter — repetition allowed

Choose two scoops from four flavours A, B, C, D. Can you choose AA? Are AB and BA the same? Use (n+r−1) nCr r, with n=4 and r=2.

4. Order does not matter — repetition not allowed

Choose two students from A, B, C and D for a team. Is AB the same team as BA? Use 4 nCr 2.

5. Multiplication principle

Choose one of 3 shirts, one of 2 pairs of trousers and one of 2 pairs of shoes. Calculate 3 × 2 × 2.

6. AND — multiplication

Choose one drink from 3 choices AND one sandwich from 4 choices. How many complete lunches are possible? Calculate 3 × 4.

7. OR — addition

Choose exactly one snack: one of 4 biscuit types OR one of 3 fruit types. Calculate 4 + 3. If two categories overlap, how should you avoid counting shared outcomes twice?

General rule: n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B).

Reflection

Explain how you decide between permutations, combinations, multiplication, and addition.
